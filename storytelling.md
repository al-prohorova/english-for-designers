# art re use — Rebranding Presentation Talking Points

## Slide 1 — Introduction
- So this project is a rebranding proposal for *art re use*, which is a Prague-based non-profit.
- They collect materials from galleries, theaters, film productions, exhibitions, and events, and instead of throwing everything away, they redistribute it to artists and cultural spaces.
- What I really liked about the project is that it’s not just about recycling — it’s about giving materials a second creative life.
- I wanted the branding to feel artistic, fresh, and community-oriented, not like a typical environmental campaign.

---

## Slide 2 — Slogans
- I worked mainly with two slogans:
  - “Umění nekončí” — “Art never ends.”
  - “Materiál pro vizi” — “Material for vision.”
- The idea behind both is that creativity continues even after an exhibition or production is finished.
- A piece of wood, fabric, or installation element can become part of something completely new.
- I wanted the tone to feel hopeful and inspiring instead of overly corporate or activist.

---

## Slide 3 — Logo
- For the logo, I experimented with a very simple typographic system.
- I liked the idea that the logo almost looks reconstructed or rearranged, because that reflects the whole philosophy of reuse.
- The fragmented layout also makes it feel flexible and playful.
- I wanted something that could work equally well on Instagram, posters, tote bags, or presentation slides.
- The simplicity also gives space for the content and the community itself.

---

## Slide 4 — Visual Identity
- Overall, I tried to create an identity that feels contemporary and creative but still clean and functional.
- A lot of sustainability branding looks very predictable, usually green and minimal in the same way.
- I wanted this to feel more connected to art spaces and creative culture in Prague.
- So the design language is bold, slightly experimental, but still easy to navigate.

---

## Slide 5 — Colors
- The color palette mixes black and white with really bright accent colors.
- Black and white reference gallery spaces and typography.
- Then colors like yellow, green, and pink add energy and make the brand feel alive.
- I also wanted the social media visuals to stand out immediately while scrolling.
- The colors help separate different categories of posts and keep the feed dynamic.

---

## Slide 6 — Social Media
- A huge part of this rebrand was thinking about how the organization communicates online.
- I designed example Instagram posts, stories, statistics, behind-the-scenes content, and educational posts.
- I wanted the account to feel active and inspiring, not just informative.
- A lot of people care about sustainability, but visual storytelling makes them actually engage with it.
- So I focused on combining strong visuals with simple messaging.

---

## Slide 7 — Impact and Statistics
- One thing I found really important was showing actual impact.
- The organization already saved hundreds of tons of material from ending up as waste.
- I used statistics because they make the environmental impact more tangible.
- But I also tried to present them in a visually engaging way, so they don’t feel dry or corporate.
- The idea was to show that sustainability in culture can have real measurable results.

---

## Slide 8 — LinkedIn and Partnerships
- For LinkedIn, the communication becomes a bit more professional.
- Here the goal was speaking directly to institutions, galleries, production companies, and possible partners.
- I wanted to explain the process very clearly:
  - instead of paying for disposal,
  - organizations can donate materials,
  - and those materials continue being used in the cultural sector.
- So the branding also positions art re use as a practical system, not just an artistic idea.

---

## Slide 9 — Campaigns and Merch
- I also explored campaign ideas and merchandise.
- I used humor and references to art history, like Duchamp or Christo and Jeanne-Claude.
- I thought this approach fits the audience because it connects sustainability with art culture in a more playful way.
- The merch and campaign visuals help make the identity feel like an actual community, not just an organization.
- I wanted the whole project to feel culturally aware and visually memorable.

---

## Slide 10 — Conclusion
- Overall, this project was about creating a stronger identity for an organization that already does something really valuable.
- I wanted the branding to communicate creativity, sustainability, collaboration, and optimism at the same time.
- The whole concept is based on the idea that materials — like ideas — can continue evolving instead of disappearing.
- And honestly, I think that’s what makes art re use interesting: it transforms waste into possibility.
